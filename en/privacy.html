Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 13, 2026

NomNom (“the app”) is a food diary for parents whose babies are starting solids. The app is developed by Fedor Semenchenko (“we”, “us”), who is responsible for processing the data described here. This policy explains what data the app handles, why, and what choices you have.

In short

  • There's no account. Your baby's diary, profile and photo are stored only on your iPhone — we don't receive them.
  • To improve the app, we collect usage statistics and crash reports through Google Firebase. They don't contain names, photos, notes or dates of birth and aren't linked to your identity.
  • No ads, no tracking, and we never sell data.

1. Data that stays on your iPhone

Everything you enter is stored in the app on your iPhone:

  • your name and your child's name, date of birth and, if you add them, sex and photo;
  • the food diary: tastings and their dates, whether your baby liked a food, notes, allergen trials and app settings.

NomNom widgets read this data on the same iPhone. We don't receive it and don't pass it to anyone. If iCloud Backup or backups to a Mac or PC are turned on, iOS includes the app's data in your iPhone backup; Apple's privacy policy applies to it.

The camera and photo library are used only when you choose to add your child's photo. The app gets only the photo you pick and saves it inside the app. Reminders are scheduled on your iPhone itself, without a server.

2. Usage statistics (Google Analytics for Firebase)

To understand which features are useful and what to improve, the app sends usage events:

  • which screens are opened and which actions are taken — for example, a tasting logged, an allergen trial marked, a serving guide opened, a reminder turned on or a widget tapped;
  • event details, for example: technical keys of foods and allergens (such as “broccoli” or “egg”), whether a tasting was the first one, a liked/disliked mark, whether notifications are allowed and whether the profile has a photo (not the photo itself); for search, only the length of the query and the number of results;
  • summary properties: the child's age in full months, the approximate number of foods tried (a range such as 11–30), the number of introduced allergens and which widgets are added.

Firebase also automatically collects technical data: a random app instance identifier, device model, iOS and app versions, language, approximate location (country, region and city) determined from the IP address, and the time of the first launch and of sessions.

We don't collect names, dates of birth, photos, the text of notes or searches, email addresses or the advertising identifier (IDFA), and we don't track you across other companies' apps and websites. Detailed statistics are kept for up to 14 months and then deleted automatically; aggregated reports without identifiers may be kept longer.

3. Crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics)

If the app crashes, a report is sent so we can find and fix the error: technical details of the crash, device model, iOS and app versions, free memory and storage, the last app events before the crash, and a random installation identifier. Reports are kept for 90 days.

4. Who processes the data

Usage statistics and crash reports are processed by Google LLC as our service provider under the Firebase terms. Under the Firebase Data Processing and Security Terms, Google protects this data at least as well as described in this policy and uses it only to provide the service to us. Google may process it on servers in the United States and other countries; transfers from the EEA, the UK and Switzerland rely on the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and standard contractual clauses. Learn more in the Google Privacy Policy and Privacy and Security in Firebase. We don't sell data and don't share it for advertising.

5. Legal basis

If you live in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland: we process usage statistics and crash reports based on our legitimate interest in keeping the app reliable and improving it (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR). This data doesn't identify you: we look at statistics only in aggregated reports and use crash reports only to fix errors.

6. Children

NomNom is intended for parents and caregivers, not for children. Information about your child stays on your iPhone; usage statistics include only the child's age in months and the food and allergen events described in section 2, none of it linked to your identity.

7. Your choices and rights

  • Deleting the app deletes all the data it stored on your iPhone.
  • Usage statistics and crash reports aren't linked to your identity, so we usually can't find the records that are yours; they're deleted automatically after the periods above. To stop sending them, delete the app.
  • Depending on where you live (for example, under the GDPR or California law), you may have the right to access, correct or delete your data, to object to processing and to lodge a complaint with a data protection authority. Write to us at nomnom.questions@gmail.com and we'll reply within one month.

8. Security

Data on your iPhone is protected by iOS, which encrypts it when a passcode is set. Statistics and crash reports are sent to Google over an encrypted connection (HTTPS).

9. Changes to this policy

If the way the app handles data changes, we'll update this page and the date above. We'll mention significant changes in the app's update notes.

10. Contact

Questions about privacy: nomnom.questions@gmail.com.
